# S009 — Where Is the Toilet?

## Seed

ask-where-something-is (extension with concrete noun)

## Circumstance

You need to find the toilet / restroom. You want a simple, polite way to ask where it is.

## Communicative objective

Be able to ask where the toilet is using a concrete noun instead of a demonstrative.

## Core phrase

トイレはどこですか。 (toire wa doko desu ka)

## Meaning

Where is the toilet? / Where is the restroom?

## Breakdown

### Vocabulary

- トイレ (toire) — toilet / restroom (from English “toilet”, very common in Japan)
- は (wa) — topic particle (already known)
- どこ (doko) — where (already known)
- です (desu) — polite copula (already known)
- か (ka) — question particle (already known)

### Structure

トイレ + は + どこ + です + か。

This is exactly the same frame as これはどこですか, only the demonstrative is replaced by a concrete noun.

### Pronunciation

- トイレ (to-i-re)

Smooth unit: **toire wa doko desu ka**

### Context

- Extremely useful in stations, restaurants, shops, parks, etc.
- Polite and clear.
- One of the highest-value survival questions a beginner can learn.

## Variations

1. トイレはどこですか。 (toire wa doko desu ka) — standard version (recommended)
2. すみません、トイレはどこですか。 (sumimasen, toire wa doko desu ka) — with polite attention-getter
3. おトイレはどこですか。 (o-toire wa doko desu ka) — slightly more polite (the お prefix adds politeness)

## Practice summary

Observed, broken down, pronounced, recalled, varied (standard / with sumimasen / with お), used in situation, and chained with attention + thanks.

## Proof of capability (completed)

User demonstrated:

- Correct recognition and meaning
- Clear understanding of the difference between demonstrative and concrete noun
- Accurate production of the core phrase
- Correct use of all three variations
- Natural choice of the more polite おトイレ + thanks in situation
- Clean short chain: attention → question → thanks

## Before

I could ask where “this” or “that” was, but I had no concrete high-value noun ready for a real survival need.

## After

I can ask where the toilet is with トイレはどこですか。 (toire wa doko desu ka) and combine it with attention-getting and thanks. I can also choose the more polite おトイレ form.
