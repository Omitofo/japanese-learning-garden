# S002 — Basic Repair

## Seed

basic-repair

## Circumstance

Someone is speaking to you in Japanese and you do not understand what they said. You need a simple, polite way to signal that and keep the interaction alive.

## Communicative objective

Be able to say that you do not understand, so the other person can adjust (repeat, slow down, use gestures, etc.).

## Core phrase

すみません、わかりません。 (sumimasen, wakarimasen)

## Meaning

Sorry, I don’t understand. / Excuse me, I don’t understand.

## Breakdown

### Vocabulary

- すみません (sumimasen) — excuse me / sorry (polite)
- わかりません (wakarimasen) — I don’t understand / I don’t know (polite negative of わかる)

### Structure

すみません、 + わかりません。

You can also say only わかりません。 (wakarimasen) if the situation is already clear, but adding すみません makes it softer and more polite.

### Grammar / pattern

- わかる (wakaru) = to understand / to know
- わかりません (wakarimasen) = polite negative present

You only need the ready-made form for now. No need to conjugate other verbs yet.

### Pronunciation

- すみません (su-mi-ma-sen) — fairly even, soft
- わかりません (wa-ka-ri-ma-sen) — five syllables, clear and steady

Say it as one polite unit: **sumimasen, wakarimasen**

### Context

- Extremely useful in any real interaction when you get lost.
- Polite and safe with strangers, shop staff, etc.
- Often followed by the other person repeating more slowly, using gestures, or switching to simpler language.

This phrase buys you time and keeps the conversation from dying.

## Variations

Keep them few:

1. すみません、わかりません。 (sumimasen, wakarimasen) — full polite version (recommended default)
2. わかりません。 (wakarimasen) — shorter, still clear
3. すみません。 (sumimasen) — just “excuse me / sorry” (useful when you need a moment)

(Later we can add “please say it again” or “please speak slowly” as extensions of this same seed.)

## Practice

1. **Observe**  
   Read: すみません、わかりません。 (sumimasen, wakarimasen)

2. **Break it**  
   すみません (sumimasen) … わかりません (wakarimasen)

3. **Pronounce**  
   Say the full phrase several times out loud until it feels smooth.

4. **Hide**  
   Look only at the meaning “Sorry, I don’t understand” and produce the Japanese.

5. **Situation**  
   Imagine someone just said a long sentence you didn’t catch.  
   Look at them and say the phrase.

6. **Link with previous seed**  
   You asked for something with これください (kore kudasai).  
   The clerk answers with a long sentence.  
   You reply: すみません、わかりません。 (sumimasen, wakarimasen)

## Proof of capability

Can you:

- Recognize the phrase?
- Say what it means?
- Produce すみません、わかりません。 (sumimasen, wakarimasen) without looking?
- Use the shorter version わかりません。 when appropriate?
- Insert it into a simple interaction after a request?

## Before

When I didn’t understand, I had no simple Japanese way to signal it and keep the interaction going.

## After

I can politely say that I don’t understand using すみません、わかりません。 (sumimasen, wakarimasen) and give the other person a chance to help me.
