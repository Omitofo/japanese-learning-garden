# S008 — Thank You

## Seed

basic-politeness / thank-you (L00)

## Circumstance

You have just received help, an answer, an object, or any small service. You want to close the interaction politely.

## Communicative objective

Be able to thank someone in a simple, polite way.

## Core phrase

ありがとうございます。 (arigatou gozaimasu)

## Meaning

Thank you. / Thank you very much. (polite)

## Breakdown

### Vocabulary

- ありがとう (arigatou) — thank you
- ございます (gozaimasu) — polite ending that makes the expression more formal/respectful

### Structure

ありがとうございます。

This is a fixed polite expression. You do not need to break it further for now.

### Pronunciation

- ありがとう (a-ri-ga-tou) — the final “ou” is a long sound
- ございます (go-za-i-ma-su)

Smooth unit: **arigatou gozaimasu**

### Context

- Safe and appropriate in almost any situation with strangers, staff, teachers, etc.
- Use after receiving something, getting an answer, being helped, or at the end of a short interaction.
- Very high frequency in daily life.

## Variations

Keep them few:

1. ありがとうございます。 (arigatou gozaimasu) — standard polite version (recommended default)
2. ありがとう。 (arigatou) — shorter, more casual (fine with friends or when the situation is already relaxed)
3. すみません、ありがとうございます。 (sumimasen, arigatou gozaimasu) — when you want to combine apology/attention + thanks

## Practice

1. **Observe**  
   Read: ありがとうございます。 (arigatou gozaimasu)

2. **Break it**  
   ありがとう … ございます

3. **Pronounce**  
   Pay attention to the long “tou” in arigatou.  
   Say the full phrase several times until smooth.

4. **Hide**  
   Produce it from the meaning “Thank you.”

5. **Chain with previous seeds**  
   Practice closing real sequences:  
   - これください。 → (receive it) → ありがとうございます。  
   - これは何ですか。 → (get answer) → ありがとうございます。  
   - すみません、わかりません。 → ゆっくり話してください。 → (they help) → ありがとうございます。

6. **Situation**  
   Someone just helped you or answered your question.  
   Close the interaction with thanks.

## Proof of capability

Can you:

- Recognize the phrase?
- Say what it means?
- Produce ありがとうございます。 without looking?
- Use the shorter ありがとう when appropriate?
- Insert it naturally at the end of a survival or information chain?
